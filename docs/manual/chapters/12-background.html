What happens in the background

This chapter explains how the system turns an AR report into letters, calls and fees. None of it needs doing by hand. It is here so you can answer "why did this tenant get that letter" and check the system is doing what MES expects.

The 9am run, step by step

Every morning at 9am Singapore time the system runs on its own, whether or not anyone is signed in. In order, it:

  1. Reads the newest saved AR report, the settings, every letter already sent, every promise to pay, and any letters edited for a tenant.
  2. Checks how old the report is. If it is more than 16 days old, it carries on and notes a warning. If Settings says to stop on an old report, it stops here.
  3. Works out which letters fall due today, one bill at a time (section 12.2).
  4. Leaves out tenants who have already had that letter for that bill, tenants with a promise to pay that has not fallen due, and tenants who owe nothing or are in credit.
  5. Puts each tenant's letters for the day into one email, using the edited wording where someone has saved an edit.
  6. Sends the tenants with no email address to the By hand list instead.
  7. Records each letter as written, then sends it from the active mailbox, in small batches. A letter is marked sent only once the mail server accepts it.
  8. On the 16th, raises the late payment fees (section 12.3).
  9. Records what it did on the 9am runs tab of the Activity Log. Anything it could not do appears in Needs attention.

If there is more to send than fits in one go, follow-up runs at 10am, 11am and noon carry on from where it stopped.

How a reminder date is worked out

Each billing date has its own clock. The first reminder goes 23 days after the billing date, and the final notice 37 days after it. Here is one tenant with two bills.

BillBilledAmountFirst reminderFinal notice
Monthly bill15 AugS$5,0007 Sep, asks for S$5,00021 Sep, asks for S$5,000
Ad hoc bill3 SepS$20026 Sep, asks for S$20010 Oct, asks for S$200

If both bills fell due the same letter on the same day, the tenant would get one email asking for S$5,200. If the tenant pays the August bill, the next report no longer has it, and the September letters ask only for what is left.

Three settings change the picture (Settings, Reminder timing):

  • Count from the newest bill only. Instead of one clock per billing date, only the tenant's newest bill starts the clock, and the letter asks for the whole balance.
  • Days of grace. Normally a letter goes on its day only. With grace days set, a letter the run missed still goes on the days after.
  • Final notice without a first reminder. On by standard, so a bill first seen after its first reminder day still gets its final notice. Turned off, the final notice waits for a first reminder that was really sent.

How the late fee is worked out

On the 16th, every tenant with a bill more than 14 days past its due date, and still unpaid, is charged S$100 before GST, once. The due date is the one in the report, or 14 days after billing if the report has none.

BillDueOn 16 September
Billed 15 August29 August18 days past due, so charged
Billed 1 September15 September1 day past due, so not yet. Charged on 16 October if still unpaid.

A tenant is not charged if they pay by GIRO (they have a Rejected GIRO Fee line in the report), if they are in credit, or if they have already been charged this month. Settings can count the 14 days from the billing date instead of the due date.

How charges are sorted into types

Each line of the AR report has a description, such as "Service & Conservancy Charges". The rules under Settings, Charge types are checked in order, and the first that matches decides the type. A line that matches none is Other Charges. After every upload, any description that landed in Other Charges is listed by name, so a new kind of charge is noticed straight away. The types are used for the charge type table on Outstanding Balances, the reports, and to recognise GIRO tenants and 1FM Maintenance tenants.

What happens when a report is replaced

  • Everything is recalculated from the new report: balances, aging, who is due a letter or a call, and the fee list.
  • A tenant not in the new report has paid in full. Their letters stop and their promises are marked paid.
  • Work already done is kept: letters sent, calls, promises, fees raised and addresses typed in by hand.
  • A letter already sent for a bill is not sent again because a new report still has that bill.
  • The same report date replaces. A corrected export with the same date takes the place of the first one.

Sending safely

A letter reaches a tenant only when three things are true: a mailbox is active, Microsoft or Google still accepts it, and sending is switched on. Each one is shown on Settings, Mailbox. Sending has three modes, set by the system administrator:

ModeWhat happens
Nothing is sentLetters are written and recorded, and no email leaves.
Test addresses onlyOnly addresses on the test list receive mail. Letters to anyone else are written, held back, and recorded as held back.
Sending to real tenantsLetters go to the tenants' own addresses. This is the live setting.

Every letter is recorded before it is sent and marked sent only afterwards, so the Activity Log always shows exactly what went and what did not.

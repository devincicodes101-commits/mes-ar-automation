MES Group · Accounts Receivable

One month, one tenant, six ways it ends

MES Group rents dormitory rooms to companies in Singapore, and some of them pay late. This is how chasing that money works month to month, where the build has got to, and what we still need from MES. Written to be read cold, with no prior knowledge of the project.

Start here

What this is about

MES Group runs worker dormitories in Singapore. Construction and marine companies rent rooms there for their staff, and MES invoices those companies every month. Roughly a hundred and fifty of them, across four dormitories.

Some of those companies pay late. Chasing them is a real job, and today it is one person’s job, done entirely by hand.

What she does now

Several times a month she downloads a list of who owes what from the accounting system. Then she works out how late each charge is, sorts it by dormitory and by type, writes reminder letters by mail merge, phones people, writes down what they said, and emails each account manager a spreadsheet of their own late customers.

Every month. Several times a month. All of it by hand.

What we are building

Software that does the repetitive part. She uploads the same file she already downloads, and the system works out who is late, drafts the reminders, ranks who to call first, keeps a record of every call and promise, and produces the reports.

She still decides who to chase and still clicks send. Nothing goes out on its own.

The one number that explains the whole product

A company owing $1,590 tells you almost nothing. That same $1,590 might be one invoice from this month, or it might be four unpaid months stacked up with the biggest and oldest at the bottom.

Those are completely different phone calls, and today she has to spot the difference herself by reading across a spreadsheet. Splitting every balance by how old it is turns out to be most of the value.

The words

Nine terms that appear below

Most of these are either Singaporean or MES’s own shorthand. None of them are technical.

Dormitory
Purpose-built housing for foreign workers. MES runs four: JPD1, JPD2, BSD and LEO. A company can rent at more than one, and owes at each separately.
Tenant
The company renting the rooms, not the worker living in them. MES bills the employer.
GIRO
Singapore’s direct debit. The tenant signs a form once and the bank collects automatically each month. Most tenants pay this way.
Ageing, and the age bands
How overdue a charge is, grouped into Current, 30 days, 60 days, 90 days and more than 90 days. Each band is roughly a month older than the last.
1FM
MES’s maintenance side. Repairs, sick bay use, putting a room back in order after someone leaves. Billed separately from rent and handled by a different team.
Relationship manager, or RM
The MES salesperson who owns a set of tenant companies. Each one gets a monthly list of their own late customers and is expected to chase them.
The AR report
Accounts receivable means money owed to MES. The AR report is the spreadsheet listing every unpaid invoice, exported from their accounting system. It is the one file the whole system runs on.
NetSuite
The accounting system MES already uses. We do not replace it or connect to it. A person exports a file from it, and a person loads the finished file back in.
Live and Terminated
Whether the tenant is still renting. A terminated tenant has moved out, which does not mean they have paid.
Where we are

What works, what waits, what has not started

Three lists. The first is finished. The second is finished but cannot be switched on yet. The third has not been begun.

this works today

Reading the report

The officer uploads the file she already downloads. The system reads it, works out how late every charge is, and sorts each one into what it is for — rent, tax, maintenance, parking, deposit.

It reads their newest file too. MES have changed the layout twice and renamed a tab three times. It copes with both.

Deciding who to chase first

Everyone who owes money is put in order, with the oldest and most persistent at the top. A company late four months running comes above one who owes more but has never been late.

The reminder letters

MES’s own two letters, word for word, with the company name and the amount filled in. They go out on their own on the 7th and the 21st, which is what MES asked for.

They can be switched back to a reviewed batch instead, if MES change their mind.

The $100 late fee

Set from MES’s own letter: a flat $100 before GST if payment has not arrived by the 15th. The system works out who that applies to from the dates on the invoices.

Calls and promises

A call list for the 7th and the 21st. Nobody drops off it after one call, and the number of times each tenant has been rung is shown, so a fourth call looks different from a first.

A promise to pay can be recorded from a phone call or from a reply the officer read in her inbox, which was not possible before.

Two of the three reports

The trade breakdown for management is finished and produces real figures.

The manager report is now built to the exact layout MES sent us. Ten of its thirteen columns are filled. The other three say plainly what they are waiting for.

The file that goes back to NetSuite

Every email, call and promise, one row each, ready to load back into the accounting system. What is in it has been written up and sent to MES for confirmation.

built, but waiting on something

Proper storage

waiting on: a login screen

Everything is currently kept in the web browser on one machine. Close it the wrong way and it is gone; two people get two separate sets of records.

The real storage is built and tested. It handles being uploaded to several times a month and it cannot lose a phone call. Nothing uses it yet, because the security correctly refuses to hand data to somebody who has not signed in, and there is no sign-in screen.

Emails actually leaving

waiting on: Outlook

The system writes every reminder and records that it went. Nothing is actually sent yet. That needs access to MES’s email account.

What each charge was for

waiting on: MES

We can say what every tenant owes in total. We cannot yet break it down into rent, maintenance and so on, because the file MES sent has every amount blank — 169 rows, no figures.

The deposits report

waiting on: MES

Built, and produces an almost empty sheet. Their whole file contains deposit figures for two tenants out of fifty three. Every tenant pays one, so the rest live somewhere we have never been sent.

Two columns on the manager report

waiting on: MES

Security Deposit needs the figures above. Risk Exposure needs a formula: we checked every row of their example and could not work out how it is calculated, and two of the numbers are negative.

not started

The login screen

ours to build · unblocks the rest

Nothing else can move until this exists. The roles are already built and tested — a manager can only see their own tenants, and only credit control can send anything — but they need somebody signed in to apply to.

Connecting the screens to the storage

ours to build · the largest piece left

Needs the login first. After that, records stop living in one browser and start being shared properly.

Three handover documents

ours to write · due at final testing

The proposal promises five things at handover. The code and the NetSuite file format are done. Still to write: a user manual, a design document, and installation notes.

They need nothing from MES, and they are as contractual as the software itself.

The shortest honest answer

The thinking works. Read the file, work out who is late, decide who to chase, write the right letter, keep a record. All of that is built and tested against MES’s own data.

It cannot be used as a real system yet, because there is no way to sign in and no email actually leaves. Both of those are ours to build and neither is waiting on MES.

Source files

The eight files, one by one

MES has sent eight things. Four are clean and moved us forward. Four have gaps. Every gap turns into a question rather than a piece of work.

these four are clear

The AR report

four tabs · JPD1, JPD2, BSD, LEO

One tab per dormitory. Every tenant with what they owe split across five age columns, a grand total, and an Update column where Jacqueline types notes after a phone call.

This is the file the system actually runs on today. Every balance on every screen comes from here. It is clean and we have had no trouble with it.

The client contact list

110 companies · 228 addresses

This unblocked the whole reminder feature. We had eight addresses out of fifty three. The reminder screens were built and could reach almost nobody.

One catch worth knowing: the addresses arrive in several different formats, sometimes several in the same cell. 

aeoncontractor@gmail.com; Admin Finance <admin@aeoncontractor.com>
AKR engg <akrpteltd@gmail.com>
Kameswaran <kamesh@appali.com>; Appali Account <accounts@appali.com>

So we have to pull the addresses out rather than use the text as it stands. Not difficult, but it has to be right — a mangled address means a reminder that silently never arrives.

The two reminder letters

first reminder and final notice

The real letters MES sends today, replacing wording we had written ourselves.

They settled the late fee question. We had two conflicting answers for months. Their own letter says it plainly:

“if payment is not received by the 15th day of each calendar month, an administrative fee for late payment amounting to $100.00 (before prevailing GST) will be charged.”

It also mentions a $50 fee for paying by cheque, which nobody had told us about. And the letters only need two things filled in — the company name and the total owed — which makes them far simpler to build than we expected.

plus the Flow and Formula tabs

We checked this against all 173 rows in their file and it matches every one. So their logic is now written down rather than guessed at.

these four have gaps

Finance AR Download

169 invoice lines · no amounts

This should be the most valuable tab in the file. Right now it is unusable.

It lists every unpaid invoice line and what each one was for — rent, VAT, maintenance, parking, deposit. That is how the system knows what a balance is actually made of.

Every single amount is blank. All 169 rows.

The detail that makes this worth reporting: four credit note rows sitting in the same tab do have amounts. So the column is not missing and the export is not broken in general. It looks like a filter that was left on and only hid the invoice rows.

This is the one thing that genuinely stops us — and we think we know why it happened.

The two manager mockups

Harry’s and Ray’s · they disagree

These were genuinely useful. They answered a question open since June: where do the security deposit figures come from? The answer is that they are a column on the manager report. It is one report, not two.

But there are two problems.

First, the files disagree with each other.

Ray's:    ...plus Overdue Total, Update, Security Deposit, Risk Exposure

Same report. Nine columns versus thirteen. Somebody has to say which one is real.

Second, two of the columns have no formula. Overdue Total and Risk Exposure are typed-in numbers that follow no rule we can find, and two are negative.

A risk exposure of minus three thousand does not obviously mean anything. Being a mockup, invented numbers are fine — but it does mean we cannot work the formulas out from the examples.

Five report placeholder tabs

SD / PF / LP / SD / RM · all empty

The workbook has tabs named Security Deposit(SD), Parking Fee(PF), Late Payment(LP), Stamp Duty(SD) and RM.

All five are completely empty. Not sparse. Zero cells with anything in them.

Their purpose is to name the reports MES wants, because their workflow says every upload should group results by dormitory and then by these categories. So they tell us what to build, but not what goes inside.

Also worth noting: “SD” is used twice, once for Security Deposit and once for Stamp Duty. Their own tab names do it. That needs clearing up before we build a dropdown from the list.

The revenue categories list

promised, not sent

MES listed this in their own workflow document as one of four things they would provide. The other three have arrived. This one has not.

It is their official list of charge types — what counts as rent, what counts as maintenance, and so on.

We have built our own by reading 345 real descriptions, and it classifies every single one of them. But it is our reading of their categories, not their list. These names end up on their reports and on their tenants’ statements, so they should confirm them.

One thing to sort out before it spreads

The contact list has real company names and real email addresses in it. Everything MES sent before this was anonymised.

That changes where the file should be kept and who should have a copy. Worth deciding now, rather than after it has been forwarded around.

Open questions

Nine questions, each answerable in a sentence

None of these asks MES to do a piece of work. Each one is a fact only they know. Together they unblock the reporting and the charge breakdown.

  1. Why is the amount column empty on all 169 invoice rows?

    How we got here. The file arrived twice, a week apart, and both times every invoice amount was blank. We assumed a broken export until we noticed that four credit note rows in the very same tab do have amounts. That means the column exists and the export works.

    So it looks like a filter that was left on. Knowing which is far faster than asking for a blind re-export and hoping.

  2. How are Overdue Total and Risk Exposure calculated?

    How we got here. Both appear as columns on Ray’s manager report mockup. We tried every obvious rule — total minus current, total minus deposit — against every row and none of them fits. Two of the Risk Exposure figures are negative.

    It is a mockup, so invented numbers are expected. But it means we cannot work the formulas out ourselves and have to be told.

  3. Harry’s and Ray’s mockups have different columns. Which one do we build?

    How we got here. Two mockups arrived together, both described as the manager report. Harry’s has nine columns. Ray’s has thirteen, adding Overdue Total, Update, Security Deposit and Risk Exposure.

    Whichever we pick, half of MES gets a report that does not match what they were shown.

  4. In the list SD / PF / 1FM / LP / SD / RM, which SD is which?

    How we got here. That list appears in MES’s workflow note as the grouping every upload should use. “SD” appears twice. The tab names confirm it: one tab is Security Deposit(SD) and another is Stamp Duty(SD).

    We need to know which is which before building a dropdown that lists them.

  5. Should 1FM credit notes count as 1FM?

    How we got here. MES’s own file says 1FM charges are identified by invoice number, and those numbers begin JP1FM. But we found two 1FM refunds numbered JP1CN, where CN means credit note.

    A rule based only on the JP1FM prefix would miss them, and they are money going the other way.

  6. Is credit counted from the billing date, or from the 1st of the month it covers?

    How we got here. The workflow calls the 1st the fourteen-day point. But a bill sent on 15 July is seventeen days before 1 August, not fourteen. Counting from the 1st instead makes both their figures line up exactly.

    It does not break anything today, because we read the due date printed on each invoice. But it should be stated so nobody recalculates it differently later.

  7. Should reminder letters use different bank details per dormitory?

    How we got here. The letters MES sent quote a DBS account belonging to MES & JPD Housing Pte Ltd. That is the company behind JPD1. But BSD sits under KT Mesdorm Pte Ltd and LEO under Kaki Bukit Developments, per MES’s own spreadsheet headers.

    Right now every letter would carry the same bank details. If a BSD tenant should be paying a different account, that is a real problem and an easy one to fix before go-live.

  8. Six companies are on a dormitory tab but not on the contact list. Missing, or excluded?

    How we got here. The contact list has four tabs. The three dormitory tabs hold 116 distinct companies between them, but the combined tab with the email addresses holds 110.

    Those six cannot be sent a reminder at all. If they simply have no address on file, that is fine and we will show them as phone-only.

  9. The revenue categories list has still not arrived.

    How we got here. MES listed four documents they would provide. Three have come. This one has not.

    We built our own list by reading 345 real descriptions and it classifies every one of them. But three of the names are ours, not theirs, and those names end up on reports and on tenants’ statements.

The one that matters most

If MES only answers one of these, it should be the first. Everything to do with what a balance is made of waits on the invoice amounts — the charge breakdown, the revenue split, the late fee calculation and four of the six reports.

The other eight can be answered alongside the work rather than before it.
